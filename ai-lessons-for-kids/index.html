What a lesson looks like

50 minutes, online, on your child's own computer. They drive; the coach guides.

  1. 0–5′
    Warm-up

    What happened since last time? One thing that worked, one that didn't.

  2. 5–20′
    Play and predict

    An interactive model, like the Word Machine on this page. They guess before every click.

  3. 20–45′
    Build

    They work on their own project with AI, step by step, with the coach watching and asking why.

  4. 45–50′
    Wrap-up

    One sentence on what they learned. Homework is small and practical.

    For parents: safety and rules

    Privacy first

    Kids never type their full name, school, address or photos of themselves into AI tools.

    Parents can join

    You are welcome in any lesson: sit in for the first one, join the last ten minutes, or drop in whenever you like. You are also copied on every invitation and recap.

    Honest homework

    AI is for thinking, research and feedback. Schoolwork is written by the student.

    Right tool, right age

    We use tools whose age rules allow your child to use them with your consent, on accounts you control.

    Questions parents ask

    What age are the lessons for?

    Kids and teenagers from 10 to 17. We group by age band: 10–12, 13–15 and 16–17, because a 10-year-old and a 17-year-old need different projects.

    Is AI safe for my child?

    It can be, with rules. Students never share personal details with AI tools, use accounts you control and with your consent, and you get a recap of every lesson.

    Will AI make my child lazy?

    Used badly, yes. These lessons teach the opposite: asking better questions, checking answers and building things themselves. Schoolwork stays theirs.

    Are lessons one to one or in groups?

    Both. One to one is fully personal. Small groups of 2 to 4 suit siblings or friends of a similar age.

    What does my child need?

    A laptop or desktop with a browser, headphones and 50 minutes. We set up the tools together in the first lesson.

    Can I join the lessons?

    Yes. Parents are welcome in any lesson, and many sit in for the first one. The written recap covers the lessons you miss.

    Which languages?

    English, Spanish and Italian.
