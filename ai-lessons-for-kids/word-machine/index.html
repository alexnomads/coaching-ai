The lesson

The student drives. Before every click they say out loud what they think will happen. The step cards tick off as they go.

    The big idea: a chatbot doesn't look answers up. It predicts the next likely piece of text, one piece at a time, using only what fits in its window.

    So: give it the factsIt can't know your calendar, prices or clients. Paste them in, or it will make up something that sounds right.
    So: repeat key instructionsIn long chats the start falls out of the window. Restate the brief or start a fresh chat.
    So: pick the temperatureFacts and numbers want cold. Slogans and ideas can run warm. Same prompt, different jobs.

    Your turn: build your own machine

    This is the learn-by-doing half. The student picks one process from their own work and asks Claude to turn it into a playable figure like this one. Writing the brief teaches clear prompting; fixing the first draft teaches iteration.

    brief.txt
    Build one interactive HTML page: a hand-drawn isometric machine that explains [a process from my work] to [who needs to understand it].

- One question at the top: [e.g. "Why does a circuit breaker trip?"]
- 3 to 5 buttons, each named as a verb the viewer does (Load, Press, Pour...)
- Every button changes something visible in the drawing
- A one-line status message that reacts to every click
- Numbered step cards under the drawing that light up as you go
- End with one sentence: "The big idea: ..."
- Plain colours, thick ink outlines, works on a phone

Ask me 3 questions about the process before you build it.
    Electrician
    Why does a breaker trip? Add load, watch the wire heat, click.
    Dental practice
    A patient's path: check in, chair, X-ray, billing, recall card.
    Estate agent
    Where a monthly mortgage payment goes: interest, capital, fees.
    Finance team
    How one invoice moves from approval to the bank.
    Events business
    Ticket sale to door scan: what has to happen in between.
    Teacher notes: 45-minute run sheet
    1. 0–5
      Hand over the link. The student opens it on their own screen and shares it back to you. You don't screenshare.
    2. 5–20
      Play steps 1 to 5. Rule: guess out loud before every click. Ask "why did that happen?" after each surprise.
    3. 20–25
      Link it to their real chats. Where have they seen a made-up fact, a forgotten instruction or a different answer to the same question?
    4. 25–40
      Build. They fill in the brief with a process from their job and run it in Claude. You coach up to three rounds of fixes. Don't touch the keyboard.
    5. 40–45
      Ship it. They publish and send the link to one colleague. Homework: bring back one thing the colleague got wrong, and fix it.
