Four things a real AI setup needs to do.

Not sixteen features to memorise. Four capability areas that separate "an app I open sometimes" from "a member of my team." This is the actual syllabus.

01 · Doing the work
Complete a task end to end, not one step of it.
Most people stop at "AI helped me draft this." A proper setup goes further — running the whole workflow with checkpoints, so what comes out the other side is something you can actually send, book, or file.
  • Online tasks — filling forms, booking, checking status
  • Purchases — comparing, staging, buying with approval
  • Email replies — in your voice, with the right context
  • Travel — booking, changes, check-in
02 · Working alongside you
Show up ready, not from zero every time.
The biggest single upgrade to how AI feels: giving it persistent memory of your work, and letting it run recurring things without you re-asking every time. This is the step almost everyone skips.
  • Memory — of your preferences, projects, people
  • Routines — recurring work that just happens
  • Proactive behaviour — nudges when they help
  • Restraint — waiting when the stakes are real
03 · Living in your stack
Connected to your actual tools, not a separate island.
An assistant that can only work in its own chat window solves half the problem. The version that reaches into your calendar, inbox, notes and docs is the one that actually saves you time — provided you control what it can see and what it can do.
  • Connected apps — calendar, inbox, notes, files
  • Permissions — scoped access and hard limits
  • Phone calls — making calls on your behalf where useful
  • Group work — showing up in shared threads too
04 · Judgment
Knows when to act, when to ask, when to stop.
The dimension that most cheap tutorials miss — because it isn't a feature, it's a way of setting things up. A well-configured AI knows what "done" looks like, doesn't invent facts to fill gaps, and asks before doing anything irreversible.
  • Recommendation quality — actually good, not just plausible
  • Multi-step chains — strings tasks together correctly
  • Creative output — images, video, briefs on request
  • Voice — sounds like you, not like a form

Questions people ask first.

Which AI tool do I need to be on?

Whichever you already use is a fine starting point. Most of what this teaches applies across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot — the setup work is where the leverage is, not the tool choice. If you're on none of them, we'll pick one on the diagnostic call.

Do I need to be technical?

No. Most students don't write code, and neither do I. The whole point of this framework is that "how you set AI up" is a design and workflow question, not an engineering one.

What if I already use AI a lot — is there more to learn?

Take the self-assessment above. If you scored 7 or higher, we're likely into refinement rather than foundations — worth mentioning on the diagnostic call so we spend the time on the right layer.
