AI isn't firing people. It's quietly not hiring them.

The Bank of England asked UK firms what AI is doing to their staff. The answer: almost no layoffs, but fewer new hires and fewer leavers replaced. Firms say AI has lifted their productivity by only about 0.5% in three years. Here's what that means for your job, your next hire, and how to be the person AI makes more valuable.

Short answer

In its November 2026 Monetary Policy Report, published on 5 November, the Bank of England found that AI has had close to zero effect on overall UK employment so far. Firms using AI are not firing more people. They are less likely to hire, and less likely to replace staff who leave. Firms put AI's productivity boost at only about 0.5% over three years, but expect about 0.9 percentage points a year over the next three, split roughly equally between more output and smaller workforces. For you, that means the risk isn't a sudden layoff. It's that the next opening never appears, and the people who stay absorb the work. The fix is the same for employees and owners: map your tasks, hand the routine admin to AI, and measure the hours you get back.

When clients ask me "will AI take my job?", they usually picture one dramatic day: a meeting, a box, a security guard. I've always told them that's not how it's playing out in the businesses I work with. What I see is quieter. A colleague leaves, and nobody replaces them. The work gets split among the people who stay.

This week the Bank of England put numbers on that. It's one of the clearest pictures yet of what AI is really doing to jobs, and it has practical consequences whether you're employed, freelancing or hiring.

What did the Bank of England find about AI and jobs?

The findings come from the Bank's November 2026 Monetary Policy Report, which draws on its Decision Maker Panel survey of firms and on its regional Agents' conversations with businesses. I read the coverage in The Register, Resultsense and PPC Land. In plain English:

The Register sums up the Bank's tone: there is "only evidence of a modest AI-driven rise in UK productivity growth." The Bank attributes much of the recent productivity pickup to other, cyclical factors, and treats a bigger AI boost as an upside risk rather than its central forecast.

AI isn't showing people the door. It's keeping the door closed behind the ones who leave.

Is AI replacing jobs or just slowing hiring?

For now, slowing hiring. Resultsense quotes the report: firms are "no more likely to fire". That matches what I wrote about companies rehiring the people AI replaced: cutting people first and finding out later rarely works. The more common move is cautious. When someone leaves, the manager asks whether the role can be covered another way.

Why it matters to you

  • If you're employed, your workload is the pressure point. When a leaver isn't replaced, their tasks land on you. People who can hand routine work to AI absorb that without burning out. People who can't, struggle.
  • If you're job hunting, the door is narrower. Fewer openings means each one is more competitive. Showing that you already work well with AI is becoming part of the CV.
  • If you run a business, the real decision is the next hire. Not "should I fire someone for AI?" but "before I replace this role, which parts of it could AI handle?"
  • Expect modest gains, not miracles. 0.5% over three years is small. Firms that get more out of AI usually redesign the work, not just buy the tool. I covered why in the best AI passed 12% of real expert work.
  • It's the UK, but the pattern travels. The survey is British. The mechanism, fewer replacements rather than mass layoffs, is what I see with clients in Spain, Italy and beyond.

How can I make myself more valuable at work with AI?

  1. List your week. Write down every recurring task and roughly how long it takes. Most people find 5 to 10 hours of routine admin they had stopped noticing.
  2. Sort each task into three piles. AI can do it (you check), AI can help (you lead), or human only (judgement, relationships, decisions). The prompt below does this for you.
  3. Start with the routine admin. That's exactly what the Bank's Agents say AI handles well: meeting notes, first drafts, data clean-up, status reports, inbox triage.
  4. Measure before and after. Time one task for two weeks the old way and two weeks with AI. A number like "the weekly report went from 3 hours to 40 minutes" is what your manager, or your next employer, remembers. My guide to measuring AI's value shows how.
  5. Volunteer for the gap. When a colleague leaves, offer a plan: which of their tasks you'll cover with AI, and what still needs a person. That makes you the one who solves the problem, not the one who quietly drowns in it.
  6. If you're the owner, redesign before you replace. Before posting the job ad, run the same three-pile sort on the departing role. You may still hire, but for a better-shaped job.

Copy this prompt: your AI task audit

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. It works for your own job, or for a role that has just become vacant.

AI task audit prompt 
Act as a practical operations coach. Help me work out which parts of a job AI can take over, so the person doing it gets more valuable, not more overloaded.

The role: [your job title, or the role that is becoming vacant]
The business: [what you do, team size, tools you already use]
Recurring tasks and hours per week: [list them, even roughly]

1. Ask me up to 5 questions to fill gaps. Wait for my answers.
2. Put every task in a table with columns:
   Task | Hours/week | Pile (AI does it, I check / AI helps, I lead / Human only) | Why | First AI tool or prompt to try
3. Estimate the hours I could get back per week, with a low and a high figure. Be conservative.
4. Pick the ONE task to start with this week and give me step-by-step instructions.
5. Write a 5-line note I could send my manager (or myself, if I'm the owner) proposing this plan, with how we'll measure it after 2 weeks.

Rules: no hype. Don't invent facts about my business; if you don't know, ask. Anything involving money, clients or legal decisions stays "Human only" or "AI helps".
Before

A colleague leaves. Their work is split around the team. Everyone stays late, and nobody can say what changed or what it cost.

After

You sort the role's tasks into AI, AI-assisted and human. AI drafts the reports and notes, you keep the clients, and you can show the hours saved.

SoftBank and MGX anchored the close, with Coatue and a group of UAE-based backers. Botpress breach: chatbot keys treated as exposed

Chatbot platform Botpress disclosed on 6 November that an attacker exploited a flaw in its internal authentication proxy and used a stolen employee API credential. Between 12 and 21 October the attacker read bot configurations in 16 customer workspaces and changed integration settings in two more. According to AI Weekly's summary of the disclosure, first covered by Unite.AI, Botpress blames an "LLM-jacking" crew that resells stolen AI usage, and tells affected customers to treat every credential in their bots as exposed: AI provider API keys, messaging-channel tokens and CRM logins.

How to use it: whatever chatbot tool you use, the bot holds keys to your other systems. Once a quarter, rotate the API keys it uses, set a monthly spending limit on your OpenAI or Anthropic account so stolen keys can't run up a bill, and remove integrations you no longer need. FAQ

Is AI causing layoffs in the UK?

Not much, so far. The Bank of England's November 2026 Monetary Policy Report says firms report close to zero effect on overall employment, and firms using AI are not materially more likely to fire workers. The effect shows up in hiring instead: they are less likely to hire and less likely to replace people who leave.

How much has AI improved productivity?

Less than the hype suggests. UK firms in the Bank's Decision Maker Panel estimate AI has raised their productivity by about 0.5% over three years, roughly 0.1 to 0.2 percentage points a year. They expect about 0.9 points a year over the next three years.

How much of UK growth comes from AI?

Just under a fifth of UK GDP growth since the start of 2026 comes from AI-related activity, according to the Bank. Most of that is from building and selling AI, such as software and data centres, rather than from businesses becoming more productive by using it.

Will AI replace my job?

For most people the near-term risk is not a sudden layoff. It's fewer openings and more work landing on the people who stay. The best protection is to know which of your tasks AI can do, use it for the routine ones, and be able to show the time you save.

Should a small business replace a leaver with AI?

Don't decide by default either way. Before you post the job ad, list the departing role's tasks and sort them into what AI can do, what it can help with, and what needs a person. You may still hire, but for a better-designed role.

What tasks is AI best at in an office?

Routine admin and knowledge tasks: meeting notes, first drafts, summaries, data clean-up, status reports and inbox triage. That's what the Bank's Agents say AI can automate or speed up when it's used well and at scale. Decisions, client relationships and anything legal or financial should stay with a human.
