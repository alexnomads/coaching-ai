Claude Fable 5.2 costs twice as much per word. It may be cheaper per job.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on 9 November. Fable 5.2 is 20% cheaper than the model it replaces but still costs twice as much per token as Opus 5.5, and Anthropic says it finishes coding tasks for about half the cost. Here's what changed, who gets it this week, and a simple way to work out which model is actually cheapest for your work.

Short answer

Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2 on 9 November at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, 20% less than Fable 5.1 and double Opus 5.5's $4 and $20. Anthropic says that on its own coding benchmark Fable 5.2 matched Opus 5.5's score at roughly half the cost per task, because it gets there with less work. Pro users can try it free until 16 November (up to half their weekly limits); Max and Ultra plans include it. The lesson for everyone else: stop comparing AI on price per word and start measuring cost per finished job, including your own time fixing the output.

Every week a client asks me some version of the same question: "Which AI should I pay for?" Most of them have already compared price tables. Almost none of them have timed how long they spend fixing what the AI gives back.

That's why yesterday's launch is more interesting than another "new model, new benchmark" story. Anthropic released a model that is more expensive on paper and, by its own numbers, cheaper in practice. If that holds for your work, the price table you've been using to choose tools is the wrong table.

What did Anthropic release on 9 November?

Two models built on the same base. Claude Fable 5.2 is the general release, which Anthropic describes as "best for the most ambitious, open-ended technical work", as reported by AI Weekly. Mythos 5.2 is the same model with fewer safeguards, offered only to organisations in Anthropic's Cyber Verification Program for security work. Why can a more expensive AI model be cheaper?

Because you don't pay per word of answer. You pay for every token the model reads and writes along the way, including the steps it takes, the files it rereads and the attempts it throws away. A model that thinks more efficiently can use far fewer tokens to reach the same result, so a higher price per token can still mean a lower bill per task.

And the bill is only part of the cost. The bigger number, in my experience, is the human one: the retries, the copy-pasting between chats and the 20 minutes you spend correcting a draft that looked finished. I wrote about this yesterday with the study that found people losing almost a day a week fixing colleagues' AI work. A model that gets it right in one go is cheaper even if its tokens cost more.

The cheapest AI is the one you don't have to redo.

Why it matters to you

  • Your "AI budget" is probably measured wrong. Monthly subscription price says almost nothing about value. Hours saved minus hours fixing is the number that matters.
  • One model for everything is the expensive habit. The top model on routine emails is waste. A cheap model on a messy, high-stakes task is false economy. I compared the tiers in Haiku vs Sonnet vs Opus; the same logic applies here.
  • Long, open-ended work is where the top tier earns its price. Reviewing a 200-page tender, rebuilding a messy spreadsheet model, working through a full folder of contracts: that's where fewer retries pay off.
  • Pro users have a free trial window this week. Until 16 November you can test Fable 5.2 on your real work without paying extra. That's the cheapest experiment you'll get.

How do I work out which AI model is cheapest for my work?

  1. Pick one real job you do every week. Not a test question. A real one: the weekly client report, the quote from a site visit, the tender summary. Use your actual files.
  2. Run it on two models with the same prompt and files. If you're on Claude Pro, try Fable 5.2 this week and your usual model next to it. On ChatGPT or Gemini, compare their top and mid tiers the same way.
  3. Count tries and time your fixes. Note how many attempts each needed and how many minutes you spent correcting the result. Write it down; your memory will flatter the faster-looking one.
  4. Check the usage meter, not just the answer. On a subscription, see how much of your weekly limit each run used. On the API, check the cost of each run in the console.
  5. Decide per job, then write it down. Make a one-line rule for each recurring task: "Weekly report: mid model. Tender review: top model." Put it in your Project instructions so you stop re-deciding.

Copy this prompt: the cost-per-job scorecard

Run your task on two models, then paste both outputs into a fresh chat with this prompt. It turns a gut feeling into a comparison you can repeat.

Cost-per-job scorecard 
I'm comparing two AI models on the same real task so I can decide which one to use for it from now on.

The task: [describe the job, e.g. "turn my site-visit notes into a client quote"]
What "done" means: [e.g. "I can send it to the client with no edits to numbers or scope"]

Output A (from [model name], took [N] tries, I spent [N] minutes fixing it):
[paste]

Output B (from [model name], took [N] tries, I spent [N] minutes fixing it):
[paste]

1. Score each output 1-5 on: accuracy against my notes, completeness, fit for my reader, and how much editing it still needs. Explain each score in one line.
2. List every factual error or invented detail you can find in each, quoting the line.
3. Using my tries and fixing time, tell me which output had the lower real cost, counting my time at [my hourly rate].
4. Write a one-line rule I can paste into my AI instructions: "For [this task], use [model] because [reason]."

Be blunt. If neither output is good enough to send, say so and tell me what context I should add first.
Before

You pick the AI with the lowest monthly price, use it for everything, and quietly spend Friday afternoon fixing its drafts.

After

You know, task by task, which model gets it right first time, you've written that rule into your workspace, and you only pay for the top model where it saves you time.

FAQ

What is Claude Fable 5.2?

It's Anthropic's newest top-tier Claude model, released on 9 November 2026 and aimed at ambitious, open-ended technical work. It costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output on the API, 20% less than Fable 5.1, and handles up to 1 million tokens of input.

Is Claude Fable 5.2 free?

Not on the Free plan. Pro and standard Team users can use it during the launch week until 16 November 2026, up to 50% of their weekly limits plus one limit reset. After that, Pro users access it with usage credits. Max and Ultra plans include it up to half the weekly limit.

Is Claude Fable 5.2 better than Opus 5.5?

On Anthropic's own coding benchmark it matched Opus 5.5's score at about half the cost per task, even though its per-token price is double. Those are Anthropic's figures and haven't been independently checked. For your work, the honest answer is to test both on one real task and time your fixes.

What is Mythos 5.2?

Mythos 5.2 is the same model as Fable 5.2 with reduced safeguards, available only to organisations enrolled in Anthropic's Cyber Verification Program for security work such as finding software vulnerabilities. Most people and businesses will use Fable 5.2.

How do I choose which AI model to use for a task?

Measure cost per finished job, not price per word. Run the same real task with the same files on two models, count the tries, time how long you spend fixing each output and check the usage it consumed. Then write a one-line rule per task into your AI instructions.

Is the most expensive AI model always the best choice?

No. Top models earn their price on long, messy, high-stakes work where fewer retries save real time. For routine tasks like sorting emails or summarising notes, a cheaper model that does the job reliably is the better buy.

Pay for the AI that gets it right, not the one with the lowest sticker price.
