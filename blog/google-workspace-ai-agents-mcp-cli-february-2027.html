Google just put a door on your Gmail and Drive for AI agents

From 1 February 2027, AI agents must reach Gmail, Drive, Calendar and Docs through Google's official MCP servers or CLI. Here's what changes, what it means if you use AI with Google Workspace, and what to check this week.

Short answer

On 25 November 2026, Google made its Workspace MCP servers and Workspace CLI generally available, and said AI agents must use them instead of the old REST APIs. Technical enforcement starts on 1 February 2027. Regular apps and people are not affected. The change targets AI agents that read your Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks. For you, it means safer access (permission checks, human confirmation before risky actions, an admin kill switch) and one job to do before February: find out which AI tools are connected to your Google account and what they can touch.

A few weeks ago a student told me, half proud and half nervous: "I connected an AI agent to my Gmail and Drive and now it just does things." I asked him one question. Can you tell me what it is allowed to do, and how you'd switch it off? He couldn't.

Google's announcement this week is aimed at exactly that gap. It's technical, but the consequences are very practical for anyone who lets an AI near their inbox.

What did Google announce about AI agents and Workspace?

On 25 November, Google Workspace and Google Cloud published new protections and developer tools for AI agents in Google Workspace. Three things became generally available, meaning ready for everyone to use:

WhatIn plain English
Workspace MCP serversAn official connection point that lets AI agents work with Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks. (MCP is the open protocol that lets AI connect to apps.) Google Keep is listed as coming soon.
Workspace CLIA command-line tool for agents that run on your own computer or in automations, with structured output and scoped sign-in.
Agent Gateway (Google Cloud)A checkpoint that sits in front of AI model, MCP and agent-to-agent traffic for companies running their own agents.

The rule that matters: under a new Workspace AI Tools Addendum, AI agents have to connect through the MCP servers or the CLI, not the traditional REST APIs. Google says "Technical enforcement begins on February 1, 2027." Traditional apps keep using the existing APIs. Outside agents also need their own dedicated OAuth client IDs, which is the "login" an app uses to act on your behalf. Usage quotas apply, with billing starting on 1 April 2027.

For two years we've been handing AI the keys to the inbox. Google is now installing a front door with a lock, a log and a light switch.

What protections come with the new Workspace AI agent access?

Google lists six protections in its announcement. The ones a non-technical user will notice:

  • Checks on every request. Each agent call is tested against your Workspace permissions, data-loss-prevention rules and classification labels.
  • Human confirmation for high-impact actions. Google gives examples: sending external emails, changing Drive sharing permissions and bulk edits. The agent has to ask first.
  • Visibility and revocation. Admins can view, audit and revoke agents in the admin console. Google says users will soon be able to see agent activity and revoke access themselves.
  • Data rules. The Addendum prohibits permanent raw storage of your data, model training on it, extended retention of derived data and API scraping.
  • Coming soon, not live yet: time-limited approvals, and settings that let you block external agents from specific Gmail messages or Drive files and folders.

One caveat on the coverage. Google's own wording targets AI agents, while some security press says every Workspace user will be required to connect agents this way. I'm following Google's wording. If you only use Gemini inside Gmail or Docs, nothing in your day changes.

Why should a non-developer care?

Because the tools you may already be using are probably "agents" under this rule: an AI assistant that reads your email, a scheduling bot that edits your Calendar, an automation that fills a Google Sheet. If one of them relies on the old connection method, its vendor has until February to move. If the vendor doesn't, it may simply stop working.

And because "it just does things" is not a security policy. Most of the damage I see from AI in small businesses isn't clever hacking. It's an over-permissioned tool, forgotten and still connected.

How do I get ready, starting this week?

  1. List every AI tool connected to your Google account. In your Google Account, open the security settings and look for third-party apps with access. Write down each AI tool, what it can read and what it can change.
  2. Remove what you no longer use. Every forgotten connection is a door left open. Revoke it.
  3. Ask each vendor one question. "Will you connect to Google Workspace through the official MCP servers or CLI before 1 February 2027?" Keep the answers. A tool with no answer is a risk to your workflow.
  4. Decide what needs a human. Sending external email, sharing files and deleting things should always pause for your yes. Check whether your tools do that today.
  5. If you run a team, tell your Workspace admin. Google recommends admins review connected apps and agents, configure data-loss-prevention policies and watch OAuth activity logs.

FAQ

What is changing for AI agents in Google Workspace?

From 1 February 2027, Google will technically enforce that AI agents access Workspace through its official MCP servers or Workspace CLI rather than traditional REST APIs. The servers and CLI became generally available on 25 November 2026.

What is an MCP server?

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard that lets AI agents connect to apps and data in a consistent way. A Workspace MCP server is Google's official connection point between an AI agent and apps like Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks.

Does this affect me if I only use Gemini in Gmail or Docs?

Google's announcement targets external AI agents and agent-style tools that connect to Workspace. Traditional apps keep using the existing APIs. Using Gemini inside Gmail or Docs does not require any change from you.

What happens to AI tools that still use the old Google API?

Their vendors need to move to the MCP servers or CLI before 1 February 2027. Ask each vendor whether they will, and keep a backup way of doing the task in case a tool stops working.

Will AI agents still be able to send emails or share files for me?

Yes, but Google says high-impact actions such as sending external emails, changing Drive sharing and bulk edits will require human confirmation, enforced by the MCP servers and the CLI.

When does billing for Workspace agent usage start?

Google says usage quotas apply and billing begins on 1 April 2027. Check the details with your Workspace admin or the tool vendor if you run agents at volume.
