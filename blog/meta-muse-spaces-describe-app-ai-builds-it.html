Describe the app you need. Meta's AI now builds it.

Meta has added Spaces to Muse, its personal AI agent. You describe a tracker, planner or dashboard in chat, Muse builds it, and connected apps keep it up to date. Here's what it does, what we don't know yet, and how to write a request that gets you a tool you'll actually use, in Muse or any other AI.

Short answer

On 6 November Meta added Spaces to Muse, the personal AI agent it launched in September. A Space is a small custom tool you create by describing it in chat: a gift tracker, a wedding planner, a school dashboard, a client hub. You refine it in the same conversation, pin it to the sidebar or main tab, and connect apps so it updates in the background. Meta says Spaces are available wherever Muse is; Muse passed 10 million users in the US and Canada in under two months. Pricing and the full list of connectable apps aren't public yet. The skill that matters is the same in every AI tool: describe the tool clearly, start with the least access, and test it for a week before you rely on it.

A lot of my clients run their business from a mix of WhatsApp threads, a notes app and a spreadsheet they're slightly afraid of. When I ask what they really want, it's rarely "an AI". It's a simple screen that shows what's due, who owes what and what to do next.

Until recently, getting that meant learning a no-code tool or paying someone to build it. This week Meta put a version of it inside a chat app used by millions of non-technical people. That's why I'm writing about it, even if you never open Muse.

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, launched on 8 September 2026. The new skill isn't building apps. It's describing exactly what you want a tool to do.

Can AI build an app for me without coding?

For simple personal and small-business tools, yes, and Spaces is one more sign of it. The pattern is the same one I wrote about with Gemini editing files right in the chat and AI teammates that keep a standing job: the chat window is turning into the place where your tools are made, not just where you ask questions.

The limit is your description. "Help me with my clients" gets you something generic. "A list of my active clients with the project, the next step, the date I promised it, and whether the invoice is paid, sorted by what's due this week" gets you something you'll open every morning.

Why it matters to you

  • The small tool you never got round to building is now a sentence away. An order tracker, a client list, a content calendar. If it lives in your head or in scattered messages, it's a candidate.
  • Meta is putting this where your customers already are. Meta's apps are where many small businesses talk to customers. Expect people to get used to AI tools that build themselves, and to expect the same speed from you.
  • Connected apps are the power and the risk. A Space that reads your email and calendar can keep itself current. It can also see a lot. Zuckerberg has said users choose which apps Muse can access and can disconnect them at any time. Use that.
  • Don't put your business on one app you can't export from. We don't yet know how you get your data out of a Space. Keep the master copy of anything important, like client or order data, somewhere you control.
  • If Muse isn't in your country, the skill still transfers. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all build a simple tracker or table from a good description. The request below works in any of them.

How do I build my own AI tracker or planner?

  1. Pick one job you track by hand every week. Orders to ship, clients to follow up, posts to publish, quotes waiting for an answer. One job, not your whole business.
  2. Write down what you check each time. The columns, the statuses and the one question the tool must answer at a glance, like "what do I need to do today?".
  3. Turn it into a spec with the prompt below. It makes the AI ask you the right questions first, then write a clear request you can paste into Muse, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.
  4. Build it with no connected apps first. Type in five real examples by hand. If the layout doesn't help with real data, connecting your email won't fix it.
  5. Connect the smallest set of apps it needs. One calendar or one inbox, not everything. Check what it can read before you say yes.
  6. Use it for a week, then decide. If you opened it every day, keep it and refine it. If you didn't, delete it. My guide to measuring AI's value shows how to put a number on the time saved.

Copy this prompt: spec your own AI tool

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Muse. It gives you a clear request to build from, whichever tool you use.

AI tool spec prompt 
Act as a practical product designer for a small business. Help me turn a job I do by hand into a simple tool (a tracker, planner or dashboard) that an AI assistant can build for me.

The job: [e.g. following up with clients, tracking orders, planning content]
How I do it now: [notebook, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, my memory]
How often I check it: [daily, weekly]

1. Ask me up to 5 questions to understand the job. Wait for my answers.
2. Propose the tool:
   - The one question it must answer at a glance
   - The columns or fields, with an example value for each
   - The statuses an item can have
   - How it should be sorted, and what should be highlighted
   - Any reminder or weekly summary it should give me
3. List which apps it would need to connect to stay up to date, and what each one would let it read. Mark which are optional.
4. Write the final build request in under 150 words, ready to paste into an AI tool.
5. Give me 3 things to check in the first week to decide whether to keep it.

Rules: keep it simple enough to use in under 2 minutes a day. Don't invent facts about my business; if you don't know, ask. Never ask for passwords or payment details.
Before

Client follow-ups live in WhatsApp, your notes app and your head. Every Monday you scroll back through messages to work out who's waiting on you.

After

You describe a client tracker once. It shows each client, the next step and the date you promised, sorted by what's due this week.

AWS gives its Kiro coding tool bots with their own memory

On 5 November AWS made bots generally available in Kiro Crew, its agent workspace for software development, as VentureBeat and InfoWorld reported. Each bot has a name, its own notes and workspace, and can run worker sessions, check their output and run on a schedule you describe in plain words. Permissions differ per bot: a review bot can comment but not approve or merge. Kiro's pricing page lists a free plan with 50 credits and Pro at $20 a month with 1,000. VentureBeat's caveat is fair: "A credit is not a completed task."

What it means for you: this is for developers, but the design is worth copying for any AI helper. Give it one job, its own notes you can read and edit, and the smallest permissions that let it do that job. Then check what it actually finished, not how busy it looked.

FAQ

What are Spaces in Meta Muse?

Spaces are custom tools inside Muse, Meta's personal AI agent. You describe what you want in chat, such as a gift tracker, wedding planner or school dashboard, and Muse builds it. You can refine it in the same chat, pin it to the sidebar or main tab, and connect apps so it updates in the background. Meta added them on 6 November 2026.

Is Meta Muse available in Europe?

Meta says Spaces are available wherever Muse is available. Muse's reported user numbers so far cover the US and Canada, and early coverage found Spaces in the US App Store notes on 6 November. Check the Muse app in your country. If it isn't there yet, you can build a similar tracker in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

How much do Muse Spaces cost?

Meta hasn't published a price for Spaces, and the coverage I read doesn't mention one. Treat it as unknown until Meta says otherwise.

Is it safe to connect my email and calendar to an AI agent?

It can be, if you limit it. Connect only the apps a tool needs for one job, check what it can read, and disconnect anything you no longer use. Zuckerberg has said Muse users choose which apps it can access and can disconnect them at any time. Keep client and payment data you can't afford to lose in a system you control.

What should a small business build first with an AI app builder?

One tracker for a job you already do by hand every week: client follow-ups, orders to ship, quotes waiting for an answer, or a content calendar. Start with no connected apps and five real examples, use it for a week, and keep it only if you opened it every day.
