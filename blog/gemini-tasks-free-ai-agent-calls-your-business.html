Google's free AI agent can now call your business. Who picks up?

Google just made Tasks in Gemini free in six countries. It runs errands in the background, pays with Google Pay, and phones local businesses on your customer's behalf, several at once. Here's what that means if you're the one answering the phone, and how to use it yourself.

Short answer

On 17 November Google made Tasks in Gemini, its background AI agent, free for adults in the US, Canada, India, Australia, Japan and South Korea, running on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Tasks can work through multi-step jobs on its own, phone local businesses (several at once, saying it's an AI), and in the US check out with Google Pay inside a spending cap, with a one-tap approval before anything is bought. For business owners the practical change is simple: some of the calls you get will soon come from an AI asking for prices, availability or a booking on a customer's behalf. Decide how you'll answer it, put your basic facts in writing, and try the agent yourself on supplier calls and price checks.

One of my clients runs an electrical contracting business in Sydney: eight technicians, office staff overseas, and about 70% of enquiries arriving on WhatsApp. When we connected AI to his job-management system, the first thing it exposed wasn't a technical problem. Most jobs had no client category, so before any report made sense we had to write a categorisation rulebook.

AI is very good at finding the rules a business never wrote down. On Tuesday Google gave a free AI agent to every adult in Australia and five other countries. That agent can ring a business like his, ask its questions, take notes and ring the next electrician on the list. If your answers on the phone live in someone's head, it's worth ten minutes to get ready for that caller.

What is Tasks in Gemini, and what changed?

Tasks is the agent mode inside the Gemini app, which Android Central notes was previously called Spark and required a paid Google AI plan. You flip the toggle from "Chat" to "Task", describe the job, and Gemini works through it in the background, then notifies you. Google's own announcement blocked automated readers, so the details below come from the outlets that covered it: Unite.AI, Android Central and Data Studios.

The part I'd underline is the calling. Google has had AI that books restaurant tables by phone for years. What's new is that it's free, it's an everyday mode in the main Gemini app, and it can call several businesses at the same time and come back with a comparison.

What happens when an AI agent calls my business?

Picture the customer's side. They type "find a plumber who can come Thursday morning and tell me the call-out fee". Gemini drafts a plan, they approve it, and it rings four plumbers. Each call ends up as a few lines in a summary on the customer's phone.

Your business is one of those few lines. If whoever answers says "I'm not sure, call back later", that's what the summary will say. If they give a clear answer on availability and a price range, that's what the customer reads while deciding. This is my reading of the launch, not something Google has measured, but it follows directly from how the feature works: the agent turns a phone call into a written comparison.

Three facts keep this grounded. The agent says it's an AI, so your staff will know. The customer approves the plan and any payment, so a person is still deciding. And, according to Unite.AI and Data Studios, businesses can opt out of receiving Gemini calls. I wouldn't opt out by reflex. For most small businesses, a customer's agent calling is still a customer calling.

An AI caller turns your phone manner into a written review. Make sure there's something clear to write down.

Why it matters if you run a small business

  • Your unwritten answers become visible. Prices, call-out fees, lead times, areas you cover. If they live in one person's head, an AI caller will expose it the same way building an agent exposes a missing rule.
  • Speed counts for more. When an agent can ring four businesses at once, the one that answers clearly first has an advantage.
  • You get a free assistant too. The same agent can call suppliers for quotes, watch prices and restocks, and do your errands. In six countries this now costs nothing to try.
  • The free launch skips Europe. If you're in Spain, Italy or the UK, you have a head start to prepare. Your customers abroad, and visitors from those six countries, may already be using it.

This continues a pattern I've written about this month: AI shopping agents arriving at online shops, United banning agents from booking flights, and ChatGPT letting you claim your business profile. Agents are becoming a channel your customers use. The phone is just the oldest channel they've reached so far.

How do I prepare my business for AI phone calls?

  1. Write a one-page phone sheet. Hours, areas you cover, typical lead time, price ranges or call-out fees, what you need to quote, how to book. Put it next to the phone and in your AI workspace.
  2. Decide your rule for AI callers. Will you give prices to an agent? Take a booking from one? A good default: answer factual questions, take the booking request, confirm by text or email with the actual customer.
  3. Tell your team. Whoever answers should know an AI may call, that it will say so, and that a short, clear answer is the goal. One sentence at the next team meeting is enough.
  4. Make the same facts public. The details on your phone sheet should match your website and your Google Business Profile. Agents that can't get an answer by phone will look there.
  5. Call yourself. If you're in one of the six countries, ask Gemini Tasks to call your own business with a typical question. Read the summary it writes. That's what your customers will see.
  6. Use it for your own errands. Try one supplier quote, one price watch and one recurring briefing this week. Set a low spending cap before you connect payment.

Copy this prompt: build your AI-caller phone sheet

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. It interviews you and writes the one-page sheet and the script your team can use.

Phone sheet for AI callers 
You are an operations advisor for a small service business. AI agents (like Google's Gemini Tasks) are starting to phone businesses on behalf of customers to ask about availability, prices and bookings. Help me prepare.

1. Interview me, one question at a time (max 8 questions), about:
   - what we sell and the areas we cover
   - opening hours and typical lead times
   - prices: fixed prices, ranges, call-out fees, what depends on a site visit
   - what we need to know before we can quote or book
   - how bookings are confirmed today and who can confirm them

2. Write a one-page phone sheet with short, factual answers to the 10 questions callers ask most. Mark any answer I couldn't give you as "MISSING: decide this".

3. Write a 6-line script for whoever answers when the caller says it's an AI: what we'll share, what we won't (no payment details by phone), and how we confirm a booking with the real customer by text or email.

4. List the facts that must match on our website and Google Business Profile.

Keep every answer under 25 words. Don't invent prices or policies.
Before

An AI calls, the person who answers isn't sure about the call-out fee, and your business shows up in the customer's summary as "couldn't give a price, call back".

After

The phone sheet gives a clear range, the booking request is confirmed by text with the real customer, and your summary line reads "available Thursday, fee stated, booking confirmed".

FAQ

What is Tasks in Gemini?

Tasks is the agent mode in Google's Gemini app, previously called Spark. You describe a job and Gemini works through it in the background, using apps like Gmail, Calendar and Maps, making phone calls to businesses and, in the US, checking out with Google Pay. It notifies you when the job is done.

Is Gemini Tasks free?

Yes, since 17 November 2026, for users 18 and over in the US, Canada, India, Australia, Japan and South Korea, on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Google hasn't published usage caps for the free tier. Calling, payments, texting and email features vary by country, with the most available in the US.

Can Gemini make phone calls to businesses?

Yes. Gemini can call local businesses for you, including several at once, after you review and approve the call plan. It says it's an AI on the call, and you receive a summary and transcript. Calling isn't available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana or Nebraska.

Can my business stop Gemini from calling us?

According to coverage of the launch, businesses can opt out of Gemini calls at any time. Before you do, consider that the caller is acting for a real customer. Many small businesses will do better with a clear phone sheet and a rule for confirming bookings than with an opt-out.

Can Gemini buy things without my permission?

No. In the US, Gemini stages a purchase, shows an itemised summary and waits for a one-tap approval on your device. It uses a single-use Google Pay card and is designed to stay within a spending cap you set in advance.

Is Gemini Tasks available in Europe?

Not in the free launch. The six countries are the US, Canada, India, Australia, Japan and South Korea. Coverage notes Pro and Ultra subscribers in some other countries have access, so check the Gemini app in your country.
