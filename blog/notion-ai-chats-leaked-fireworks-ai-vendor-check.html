Notion's AI chats leaked through a vendor you never heard of. Is your data next?

Attackers who broke into Fireworks AI, a company that runs AI models for other apps, took Notion AI chat transcripts from under 1% of Notion workspaces. You probably weren't hit. But the same chain sits behind most AI tools you use, and it takes ten minutes to check yours.

Short answer

Notion says chat transcripts from its Notion AI "Insights" feature were taken in a breach at Fireworks AI, an outside company Notion used to run some AI models. Fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces were affected. Notion cut Fireworks off on 3 November, contacted affected customers from 9 November, revoked its own API keys found in the chats on 11 November and reported other companies' keys to their issuers on 12 November. If Notion hasn't emailed you, it says you don't need to do anything. The bigger lesson is for everyone: what you type into an AI tool can pass through vendors you've never heard of. Never paste passwords or keys into a chat, rotate any you already have, and ask your AI tools who processes your data.

In almost every first session I run, someone shows me a chat where they pasted a password, a bank detail or a client's full contract into an AI tool "just to get it done". I get it. The chat box feels private, like a notebook on your desk.

This week showed why it isn't. Notion didn't get hacked. A company Notion paid to run part of its AI did. Most people using Notion AI had no idea that company existed, and that's the point of this post.

What happened with Notion AI and Fireworks?

According to Notion's security advisory, last updated on 13 November, Notion used Fireworks AI for two jobs: classifying chats for its Insights feature (which helps teams understand how they use AI) and running some open-weight models inside Notion. TechCrunch reports that an attacker got in through Fireworks' cloud provider, reached a Fireworks development system and took customer billing information and some datasets. Fireworks calls it its second breach in two months.

QuestionPlain-English answer

Notion's line for everyone else is clear: "If you haven't heard from us, we haven't identified your workspace as affected." Affected customers got an email and can review the exposed chats inside their workspace.

Why does an AI vendor breach matter to you?

  • Your AI tool has its own suppliers. The app you log into (Notion, a CRM, a note-taker, a website builder) often sends your prompt to another company to run the model. Security people call these subprocessors. You signed up with one company, but your words can touch three.
  • "We don't keep your data" is a promise, not physics. Notion had a no-retention commitment from its vendor, according to CSA's reading of the reporting, and transcripts were still there to steal. Contracts reduce risk, they don't remove it.
  • Secrets in chats are the real damage. A leaked chat about your marketing plan is embarrassing. A leaked chat with your Stripe key or email password is an open door. That's why Notion's first concrete action was revoking keys.
  • Deleting the chat doesn't cancel the key. As Fireworks put it, quoted by CSA: "removing a stored secret alone does not invalidate the underlying credential". You have to change it where it was issued.
  • Small businesses have the same exposure with less help. A big company has a security team reading advisories. You have you. Ten minutes of hygiene now is worth more than any policy document.
Treat every AI chat like an email to someone you haven't met yet. Useful, fast, and never the place for a password.

How do I check whether my AI chats are safe?

Here's the ten-minute routine I'm running with clients this week. It works for Notion AI, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or any app with AI built in.

  1. If you use Notion, check your inbox. Search for an email from Notion about a security incident. No email means Notion hasn't identified your workspace. If you got one, workspace owners can review the affected chats inside Notion, and you should treat them as exposed.
  2. Search your AI chat history for secrets. In each tool, search your past chats for words like "password", "API key", "sk-", "token", "IBAN" and "login". Most chat tools have a search box in the sidebar.
  3. Rotate anything you find. Change the password or regenerate the key at the source (your bank, Stripe, Google, your hosting), not just in the chat. Then delete the chat.
  4. List your AI tools and their suppliers. For every app you use with AI inside, open its trust, security or privacy page and look for "subprocessors". Write down who runs the AI and whether they keep your data.
  5. Check the data settings on your own accounts. Turn off training on your chats where your plan allows it, and set the shortest chat history you can live with. Business and team plans usually offer more control than free ones.
  6. Set one rule for your team. No passwords, keys, card numbers or full ID documents in any AI chat, ever. Use a password manager to share access, and replace client names with "Client A" when the name doesn't matter.

FAQ

Was Notion hacked?

No. Notion says its own systems were not breached. Attackers broke into Fireworks AI, an outside company Notion used to run some AI features, and took Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature from fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces.

How do I know if my Notion workspace was affected?

Notion contacted affected customers by email from 9 November 2026. Notion says that if you haven't heard from it, it hasn't identified your workspace as affected and you don't need to act now. Affected workspace owners can review the exposed chats inside Notion.

What should I do if I pasted a password or API key into an AI chat?

Change the password or regenerate the key where it was issued (your bank, Stripe, Google, your hosting provider), then delete the chat. Deleting the chat alone doesn't cancel the credential. Notion recommends this for affected customers, and it's good practice for any AI tool.

What is Fireworks AI?

Fireworks AI is an inference company: it runs AI models for other businesses' apps. Notion used it for Insights classification and to serve open-weight models. TechCrunch reports Fireworks has tens of thousands of customers and that this was its second breach in two months.

What is an AI subprocessor?

A subprocessor is an outside company that handles your data on behalf of the app you signed up with. In AI tools, it's often the company that actually runs the model. Most business apps list theirs on a trust, security or privacy page.

Is it still safe to use AI tools at work?

Yes, with simple rules. Keep passwords, keys, card numbers and ID documents out of every AI chat, replace client names with placeholders when they don't matter, use business plans with data controls where you can, and check which vendors process your data.
