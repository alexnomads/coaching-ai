OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic will honour one AI training opt-out. Should your website use it?

The four biggest AI labs have backed a single line you can add to your website's robots.txt to say "don't train your AI on my content". What it does, what it doesn't do, and how a small business should decide, because blocking the wrong thing can make you harder to find in AI answers.

Short answer

There is now one shared way to tell the big AI labs not to train on your website. On 30 October, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic said they will honour a new robots.txt token, __usage_ai-training, proposed in an IETF draft by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley. Anthropic has started rolling it out, OpenAI says in the coming months, Microsoft from 1 February 2027 and Google in 2027. It only covers training: it doesn't stop bots from visiting, doesn't remove what's already been collected, and doesn't affect Google or Bing search rankings. For most small businesses that want to be found, the smart move is to decide on purpose, not block everything.

Every few weeks a client asks me some version of "is AI stealing my website?" The honest answer has been messy: each AI company had its own crawler name, its own rules and its own small print. Blocking them meant keeping a list, and you never knew if you'd caught them all.

Yesterday that got simpler. Not solved, but simpler. And it forces a question most business owners haven't thought through: do you actually want AI to stop learning from you?

What is the new AI training opt-out token?

As AI Weekly summarised The Verge's report, the four companies are backing one shared signal for publishers. It comes from an Internet-Draft called "Machine-Readable Training Preference Signal", written by Anthropic's standards lead Kevin Kelley and dated 30 October. It's an individual draft, so it has no formal IETF standing yet, but the four biggest labs have said they'll follow it.

In practice it is two lines in the robots.txt file that already sits at the root of your website:

The draft was shaped with input from organisations including Cloudflare, News Corp, the Financial Times, the BBC, the EFF, Creative Commons and Common Crawl, according to AI Weekly. A write-up of SiliconANGLE's coverage on DEV confirms the same token and timelines.

Does blocking AI training remove my website from ChatGPT or Google answers?

No, and this is where most people will get it wrong. The token is about training: what goes into the next model. It is not about fetching: the AI visiting your page today to answer someone's question.

  • Search is untouched. According to Martin Cid Magazine's breakdown, Google says Google-Extended doesn't affect Google Search, and Microsoft says its settings don't change whether you appear in Bing.
  • AI answers are a separate switch. Microsoft has a separate "grounding" preference, set page by page with a meta tag or HTTP header, for content its AI pulls in to answer questions in Bing, Copilot and Microsoft 365. Microsoft warns that saying no there significantly reduces your visibility in those answers.
  • The past stays in. Changing robots.txt doesn't remove content already collected into datasets or trained models. It only looks forward.
  • It's a request, not a lock. OpenAI describes the token as "a coordination mechanism rather than a security control". Polite crawlers follow it. Bad scrapers don't.

Why it matters to you

For a small business, the real question isn't "can I stop AI?" It's "what do I want AI to know about me?"

If people ask ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini "who's a good accountant in Valencia?" or "best AI coach for small business", the models answer from what they learned in training and from pages they fetch live. A business that blocks training today might, over time, be known less well by the next generation of models. That's a real trade-off, and I wrote about the bigger picture in your next customer won't look at your website, it will read it.

On the other side, if your product is your content (courses, photography, paid newsletters, recipes, templates), training on it for free is a different matter. For you, this token is the first opt-out that works across all four big labs with one line.

My rule of thumb:

  • You sell a service and want clients to find you: leave training on for your public marketing pages. Being known by AI is free marketing.
  • You sell content: block training on the paid or premium parts, and keep your sales pages and "about" open.
  • Either way: don't block search crawlers or AI answer features by accident while trying to block training.

How to set your AI training preference in robots.txt

  1. Look at what you have now. Type yourdomain.com/robots.txt into your browser. Note any lines mentioning GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended or User-agent: * with Disallow: /. Some site builders and security plugins added AI blocks without telling you.
  2. Decide page by page, not site-wide. Write two lists: pages you want AI to know (home, services, about, pricing, FAQ, blog) and pages you'd rather keep out of training (paid content, client areas, downloads).
  3. Add the new group only if list two isn't empty. Use User-Agent: __usage_ai-training, then a Disallow: line for each folder you want out of training, or Disallow: / for everything. Remember the wildcard group doesn't count.
  4. Keep your old rules. Don't delete existing bot-specific lines during the changeover. Google and Microsoft won't follow the new token until 2027.
  5. Check nothing blocks search. Make sure there's no blanket Disallow: / under User-agent: * unless you truly want to vanish from search engines.
  6. Set two reminders. 1 February 2027 (Microsoft starts honouring the token) and early 2027 to check Google's progress. If you use Wix, Squarespace, Shopify or WordPress and can't edit robots.txt directly, ask whoever manages your site.

FAQ

What is __usage_ai-training in robots.txt?

It's a shared robots.txt token that tells AI companies not to use your pages to train their models. You add it as its own group, "User-Agent: __usage_ai-training" followed by "Disallow: /" or specific folders. It comes from an IETF Internet-Draft written by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley in October 2026.

Which AI companies honour the new opt-out token?

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic. Anthropic has started rolling it out, OpenAI says in the coming months, Microsoft from 1 February 2027 and Google in 2027. Meta has welcomed the idea but hasn't committed to honour it.

Will blocking AI training hurt my Google ranking?

No. The token only covers AI training. Google says Google-Extended doesn't affect Google Search, and Microsoft says its training settings don't change Bing eligibility. Just don't add a blanket "Disallow: /" under "User-agent: *", which blocks search engines too.

Does the opt-out stop ChatGPT or Copilot from quoting my website?

Not by itself. It covers training, not live fetching to answer questions. Microsoft has a separate grounding preference, set with a meta tag or HTTP header, for its AI answers, and warns that opting out of grounding significantly reduces visibility in Bing and Copilot.

Does the opt-out remove content AI has already trained on?

No. It only applies going forward. Content already collected into datasets or trained models isn't removed when you change robots.txt.

Should a small business block AI training?

It depends on what you sell. If you sell a service and want to be recommended, keeping your public marketing pages open usually helps. If your content is the product, such as courses, photos or paid articles, blocking training on those sections makes sense. Decide page by page.
