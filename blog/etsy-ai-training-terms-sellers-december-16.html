Etsy can train AI on your shop. Check where else you upload.

Etsy's new Terms of Use take effect on 16 December. They let Etsy and unnamed partners use listing photos, descriptions and reviews to train AI, under a licence described as perpetual and irrevocable. Sellers are pushing back this week. Here's what the terms say, what you can still do, and the five-question check I'd run on every platform you sell through.

Short answer

From 16 December 2026, Etsy's updated Terms of Use let Etsy and its "third party partners and vendors" use seller content such as listing photos, descriptions, reviews and shop names to develop and train AI. The licence is described in the terms as worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual and irrevocable, so deleting a listing later may not take it back. Sellers in the UK and EEA can object through Etsy's Privacy Policy; for US sellers, reporters found no general opt-out switch. Etsy says nothing changes in its existing practices. Whatever you sell on, the lesson is the same: read what you've licensed before you upload, and keep your best originals somewhere you control.

When people ask me whether AI will copy their work, they usually mean a chatbot scraping the open web. This week's question is closer to home: what did you already allow when you clicked "accept" on the platform that pays your bills?

Etsy published new terms on 26 October. Coverage built slowly, and over the last few days it turned into a proper seller backlash, with some shops reportedly closing. With 25 days left before the terms apply, it's a good moment to read them calmly, and to run the same check on every other platform you put your work on.

What do Etsy's new AI terms actually say?

I couldn't load Etsy's own legal pages in full, so the details below come from three outlets that quote the terms directly: Value Added Resource (updated 17 November), Shopifreaks (31 October) and Bernard (updated 18 November). The source documents are Etsy's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy.

WhenPublished 26 October 2026. Takes effect 16 December 2026.
Who can use your contentEtsy and its "third party partners and vendors". The partners aren't named.

Two things are still unclear in the coverage I read. Etsy hasn't said how content you already deleted is treated, and it hasn't said whether partners may use seller content to train models for purposes unrelated to Etsy.

Can I opt out of Etsy using my listings for AI?

It depends where you live. In the UK and EEA, Etsy's Privacy Policy gives you a right to object, and Bernard reports objections go through the contacts in Section 12 of the policy (Help Center, chat, or Etsy's data protection officer). Value Added Resource is more cautious and says it's not clear those rights cover creative content. In the US, neither outlet found a switch or form, and Etsy points US residents to a separate regional privacy notice.

Closing your shop is not a clean exit either. The terms say closing an account won't affect the availability of some content you posted before, and a perpetual licence doesn't end when you leave. That's why the useful decision is about what you upload from now on, not what you delete.

You can't take back what you've licensed. You can choose what you upload next.

Why it matters even if you don't sell on Etsy

  • This is becoming the norm. Platforms want training data and they already hold yours. Last month I wrote about Meta's new AI terms; this week it's Etsy. Expect the same clause in more marketplaces, design tools and social apps.
  • "Perpetual" changes the maths. A licence you can't revoke means today's upload is a decision for years, not for this season. Treat your best original photos and designs like stock you can't recall.
  • The platform keeps the door shut to others. Etsy bars outside AI companies from training on the same content. That's a reasonable protection for you, but it also means the value of your data stays with the platform.
  • Reading terms is now a business skill. Nobody reads 20 pages of legal text. AI can, in two minutes, if you ask it the right questions. That's the practical part of this post.

If you run a website as well, the same thinking applies to AI crawlers: I covered the robots.txt line that opts your site out of AI training. And the Notion vendor leak is a reminder that "partners and vendors" are real companies with their own security.

What should Etsy sellers do before 16 December?

  1. Read Sections 5 and 6 once. Open Etsy's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, or paste them into the prompt below. Ten minutes, and you know exactly what you've agreed to.
  2. If you're in the UK or EEA, decide whether to object. Use the contacts in the Privacy Policy and keep a copy of what you sent and when.
  3. If you're in the US, check the regional privacy notice. Reporters couldn't confirm what it allows. Ask Etsy support in writing, so you have an answer on record.
  4. Change what you upload, not just what you delete. Use web-sized product photos rather than print-ready files, and keep your master designs, patterns and source files off any platform.
  5. Build a home you control. Your own website and an email list are the only places where you write the terms. Even a one-page site with your best work and a sign-up form counts.
  6. Run the same check on every platform. Instagram, Shopify apps, Canva, print-on-demand services, marketplaces. Same five questions, same prompt.
  7. Set a reminder for terms updates. Platforms email you when terms change. Forward those emails to the prompt below instead of archiving them.

This isn't legal advice. If your income depends on one platform, a short call with a lawyer who knows IP is money well spent.

Copy this prompt: read any platform's AI terms in two minutes

Paste a platform's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy (or the changed sections) into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

Platform AI-terms check 
You are helping a small business owner understand a platform's terms. I am not asking for legal advice; I want a plain-English summary I can act on.

Platform: [NAME]. I live in: [COUNTRY]. I upload: [e.g. product photos, designs, videos, reviews].

Read the terms and privacy policy below and answer these five questions. Quote the exact clause (section number) for each answer:
1. WHO can use my content for AI? (the platform only, or partners, vendors, affiliates?)
2. WHAT content is covered? (photos, text, reviews, videos, my name, messages?)
3. For WHAT purposes? (their own features only, or training models in general?)
4. For HOW LONG? Is the licence perpetual or irrevocable? What happens if I delete content or close my account?
5. Can I OPT OUT or object, where I live? Give the exact steps or contact.

Then:
- List anything that changed compared with the previous version, if I've included it.
- Flag anything vague or missing with "UNCLEAR" instead of guessing.
- Finish with 3 practical steps for me, in order of importance.

Also today in AI

ChatGPT dictation now types as you talk, with no 10-minute cap

OpenAI started rolling out Live Dictation on 18 November: ChatGPT now transcribes while you speak, and the previous 10-minute recording limit is gone, Gizmodo and Digital Trends report. It's coming to all plans on iOS, Android, web and desktop. For consumer accounts that share audio, OpenAI may train on dictation audio; the switch is "Improve the model for everyone" in Settings.

How to use it: do a 20-minute brain dump of a messy project, then ask ChatGPT to turn it into a plan with next steps. Check your data controls first if you talk about clients.

Gemini can now use your Mac for you

Google's Gemini app for Mac can now navigate apps and websites in a background window to finish multi-step tasks, such as pulling details from local PDFs into a shipping form, Google announced on 19 November. It's rolling out in English in the US for Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers on Apple Silicon Macs. You choose which apps it can use, can pause it at any time, and approve key actions; 9to5Google says you start it by adding "@Computer use" to a prompt.

How to use it: give it one boring copy-paste job you do every week, allow only the two apps involved, and watch the first run before you trust it alone.

Canva adds a switch to turn off generative AI

Canva now lets users turn off its generative AI features, including Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Expand and Dream Lab, PetaPixel reports. Background Remover and Magic Grab stay on. Canva's help page says the switch is for Free and Pro accounts and is rolling out gradually, while admins manage AI on Teams, Business, Enterprise and Education plans. A separate setting shows less AI-generated content in search.

What it means for you: if a client or brand guideline says "no AI imagery", you now have a setting to point to. If you're a team admin, decide the policy once instead of leaving it to each designer.

FAQ

Is Etsy using my listings to train AI?

From 16 December 2026, Etsy's updated Terms of Use allow Etsy and its third-party partners and vendors to use seller content, including listing photos, descriptions and reviews, to develop and train AI and machine-learning technology. Etsy says this is not a change in its existing practices.

When do Etsy's new terms take effect?

Etsy published the updated Terms of Use on 26 October 2026, and they take effect on 16 December 2026.

Can US sellers opt out of Etsy AI training?

Reporters at Value Added Resource and Shopifreaks found no general opt-out for US sellers. Etsy refers US residents to a separate regional privacy notice, and what it allows hasn't been confirmed. Ask Etsy support in writing if you need a definite answer.

Can UK and EU sellers object to Etsy using their content for AI?

Etsy's Privacy Policy gives UK and EEA users a right to object, through the contacts listed in the policy. Etsy says it will stop unless it has compelling legitimate grounds to continue. Some coverage questions whether that right covers creative content.

If I delete my listings or close my shop, does Etsy lose the licence?

Not necessarily. The licence is described as perpetual and irrevocable, and the terms say closing an account won't affect the availability of some content posted before. Etsy hasn't said how deleted content is treated for training.

How can I check what other platforms do with my content?

Ask five questions of any terms: who can use your content for AI, what content, for what purposes, for how long, and whether you can opt out where you live. Paste the terms into an AI assistant with the prompt in this article and ask it to quote the exact clauses.
