ChatGPT's always-on agents reach the $20 plan. Hire yours properly.

OpenAI's "dots" keep working after you close the chat, with their own cloud computer and access to thousands of apps. Since 19 November a small group of ChatGPT Plus users is testing them, and every US Business Standard user has one. Here's what a dot is, who can use it, and the job description I'd write before switching one on.

Short answer

OpenAI has started testing dots, its always-on ChatGPT agents, with a small number of $20-a-month Plus users, and has opened them to all US ChatGPT Business Standard users. Until now they were limited to the Pro and Business Premium plans (plus an Enterprise beta) since launching on 29 September. A dot keeps working between conversations, runs on its own cloud computer, connects to apps, and follows rules you set: act alone, ask first, or never. If you get one, treat it like a new hire: give it one clear job, write its limits down, and approve everything for the first two weeks.

Every daily post on this blog starts with an AI pipeline that runs while I'm doing something else. It took me a while to learn what makes that work. Not a clever prompt. A written brief: who it writes for, which sources count, what it must never do (invent a number, a quote, a client), and when it has to stop and tell me something went wrong.

That brief is a job description. And this week the kind of AI that needs one moved from the expensive plans to the plan millions of people already pay for.

What are OpenAI dots?

OpenAI introduced dots on 29 September as persistent assistants that keep working on your goals instead of waiting for your next message. Each one gets its own cloud computer and browser, can connect to more than 4,000 apps through plugins, and can be reached in ChatGPT on desktop, web and mobile, and in Slack and Teams. One example OpenAI gives: an early tester's dot noticed an invoice that hadn't been sent, prepared it, and sent it once the tester approved.

On 19 November Thibault Sottiaux, who leads engineering for Codex and ChatGPT, said OpenAI is "starting to test dots with a small number of ChatGPT Plus users", as Startup Fortune reported. AI Weekly and Emergent covered the Business Standard expansion the same week, and The Neuron flagged it again in today's digest.

How do dots decide what they're allowed to do?

This is the part worth reading twice. According to OpenAI, dots come with built-in rules about when to act and when to ask, and you can add Custom Rules that allow an action, require your approval for it, or block it. An "auto-review" step checks actions that could affect your accounts or share information. Background research only uses read-only app tools. Changing a password always stays with you, saved passwords aren't shown to the model, and a monitoring system can pause or stop a dot's work.

OpenAI is also plain about the limit: "Dots can still make mistakes, so always review consequential work."

Why it matters to you

  • Agents are moving from demo to default. When the $20 plan gets something, it stops being a toy for early adopters. Your team, your suppliers and your competitors will have one too.
  • An agent that works while you're away needs instructions that work while you're away. In a chat, you correct it in the next message. A dot acts between your messages. Vague goals get confident, average work.
  • The approval settings are the real product. "Ask first" on anything that leaves your business is the difference between a helpful assistant and an email you can't unsend. I wrote about what happens when agents get too much access in the California case against OpenAI.
  • Not everyone can use it yet. If you're in Spain, Italy or anywhere in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, the Plus test doesn't include you. That's fine: the job description below works for any agent, including Gemini's Tasks or a Claude Project.

How do I set up my first AI agent safely?

  1. Pick one job with a clear finish line. Not "help with admin". "Every Friday at 9:00, list invoices unpaid for more than 30 days and draft a polite reminder for each." One job, one owner, one output.
  2. Connect only what that job needs. For the invoice job: your accounting tool and your email drafts. Not your whole Drive, not your bank. Add apps later when a task needs them.
  3. Write the three lists before you start. Do it alone, ask me first, never. Use the picture above as a starting point and edit it for your business.
  4. Give it your real examples. Paste two reminder emails you've actually sent and one you'd never send. Examples beat adjectives like "friendly but firm".
  5. Approve everything for two weeks. Every time you correct it, turn the correction into a rule. Loosen a limit only after it has got that action right every single time.
  6. Review weekly, in ten minutes. What did it do, what did it get wrong, what should it stop doing? The same review you'd give any new hire in their first month.

Copy this prompt: write your agent's job description

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, fill in the brackets, and use the result as your dot's instructions (or any agent's).

AI agent job description 
You are helping me write the instructions for an AI agent that will work in the background for my business, while I'm not watching. Ask me up to 5 questions first if anything below is unclear. Then write the instructions.

About my business: [what you do, who your customers are, team size]
The one job I want the agent to do: [e.g. every Friday, find invoices unpaid for 30+ days and draft a reminder for each]
How often and when: [schedule or trigger]
Apps it will need, and nothing more: [e.g. accounting tool (read only), email (drafts only)]
Two real examples of good output: [paste them]
One example of output I'd never accept: [paste it, and say why]

Write:
1. A one-paragraph role description, as you would for a new employee.
2. Three lists: DO ALONE, ASK ME FIRST, NEVER. Be specific to this job and these apps. Anything that sends, pays, deletes, books or posts publicly goes in ASK ME FIRST or NEVER.
3. A short checklist the agent runs before finishing each task.
4. What it must do when something fails or data is missing (tell me, don't guess, don't skip silently).
5. Five questions I should ask myself in a weekly 10-minute review of its work.
Before

Switch on the agent, connect every app it offers, type "help me stay on top of things", and find out on Monday what it decided to do.

After

One job, the minimum apps, three written lists of what it may do, two weeks of approving everything, and a ten-minute review every Friday.

Also today in AI

Perplexity launches a web watchdog for agents

Perplexity's new Monitor API, launched on 16 November, runs searches on a schedule (from every 15 minutes to weekly) and sends an alert only when it finds a relevant update. It can watch a topic, a company or a question across the web and news, or track up to 10 specific pages and report what changed. Web monitors cost $1 per 1,000 successful runs.

How to use it: this is a developer tool, so you'll need a tech partner or an automation tool to plug it in. The idea is worth stealing either way: decide which three competitors, suppliers or regulations you want watched, and get a message only when something changes. I built a version of this for a client in my AI competitor market watch case study.

Siri AI starts rolling out inside Apple's apps

Apple is rolling out Siri AI in its own apps in the iOS 27.3 beta, alongside a developer preview that lets other apps offer their own actions to Siri, such as checking a delivery or booking a reservation, Forkast reports. Apple says it will first ship to customers in a few months, with App Store submissions in spring, according to iClarified. Siri AI isn't available on iPhone in the EU yet.

What it means for you: if your business has its own app (bookings, orders, deliveries), ask your developer whether it's worth adding Siri actions before spring. If you don't, nothing to do yet, but your customers' phones are about to start doing tasks for them, the same trend as dots.

FAQ

What are OpenAI dots?

Dots are always-on agents inside ChatGPT, launched on 29 September 2026. Each one has its own cloud computer and browser, can connect to thousands of apps, keeps working between conversations, and follows rules about what it can do alone, what needs your approval, and what is blocked.

Can ChatGPT Plus users get dots?

Only a small test group for now. OpenAI started testing dots with some Plus users on 19 November 2026. Reports say dots appear in eligible accounts and support can't switch them on, testers must be 18 or over, and the UK, the EEA and Switzerland are excluded.

Which ChatGPT plans include dots?

Pro and Business Premium since launch, an admin-enabled beta for Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare, all US Business Standard users since 19 November 2026, and a limited test on Plus.

Are dots available in Europe?

Not on the Plus test, which excludes the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and Business Standard access is for US users. Pro and Business Premium launched in OpenAI's "eligible markets". Check your own account and OpenAI's help pages for your country.

Is it safe to let an AI agent work while I'm not watching?

It can be, with limits. Give it one job, connect only the apps that job needs, require your approval for anything that sends, pays, books or posts, and block passwords, deletions and banking. OpenAI itself says dots can make mistakes and consequential work should be reviewed.

What should my first AI agent do?

A repetitive task with a clear output and low risk if it goes wrong: a weekly list of unpaid invoices with draft reminders, a Monday summary of new enquiries, or a daily digest of news about your competitors. Avoid anything that sends money or messages without your approval at first.
