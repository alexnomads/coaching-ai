Build an AI social media team that never posts without your yes

Six agents, one job each: find ideas, write in your voice, design, schedule, report, and one manager to run them. Here's how to set it up for a small business, the rules that keep your account safe, and the one decision you should never hand over.

Short answer

You can split social media into six AI agents, each with one job: a Manager you talk to, a Scout that finds ideas, a Writer that drafts in your voice, a Designer, a Scheduler that queues posts in a posting tool, and an Analyst that reports weekly. Tools like xAI's Grok Bot (included in the $30 X Premium tier) or Claude's scheduled tasks can run them. The setup only works if three things are true: the agents learn your voice from your real posts, nothing is published until you approve it, and you never let them reply to strangers for you. Start with the Manager and the Scout, run it by hand for a week, then automate.

Every weekday morning, a LinkedIn draft lands on my phone. An AI agent has already read the morning's news, picked an angle I haven't used in three weeks, matched it to a client story I haven't told in seven days, and written the post in my voice. It even drafts the first comment with the link.

I read it. Sometimes I post it almost as it is. Sometimes I rewrite the hook. Sometimes I bin it. But the agent never posts it for me. That's not a technical limit. It's the most important design decision in the whole system.

This week Andrew, who writes AI guides on X as @s4yonnara, published a step-by-step build of a six-agent social media team on xAI's Grok Bot. It's the clearest version I've seen of something I've been running in a rougher form for the past three weeks. So I'm going to walk through his design, rebuild it for the people I coach (business owners and professionals who post on LinkedIn and Instagram, not X power users), and be honest about which part I still haven't built.

What is an AI social media team?

Doing social media well is five jobs at once: keep up with your field, find something worth saying, write it so it sounds like you, make a visual, publish at the right time, then learn from what worked. Hire for that and you have a payroll. Do it yourself and it eats the hours you meant for your actual work.

Andrew's answer is to give each job to a separate AI agent, plus one manager on top:

AgentIts one jobWhat it must never do
ManagerThe only agent you talk to. Hands work to the right specialist and brings back one clean result.Let anything go out without your approval.
ScoutEach morning, finds 5 ideas: rising topics, repeated questions, claims worth challenging, each with a source link.Invent a quote or a number.
WriterTurns the idea you pick into 3 drafts with different hooks, in your voice.Publish, or state a fact without a source.
DesignerOne visual per post, in your brand colours and fonts.Use logos, people or screenshots you didn't provide.
SchedulerQueues the approved post in your posting tool and saves the link.Write or edit copy.
AnalystEvery Monday: top 3 and bottom 3 posts, what they share, one thing to do more of and one to stop.Draw conclusions from a sample too small to mean anything.

One job per agent sounds bureaucratic. It's the opposite. When drafts are weak, you know it's the Writer's instructions. When ideas are stale, you fix the Scout. You're never debugging "the AI".

Why can't the agents just post for you?

Two reasons: one technical, one that matters more.

The technical one: on Grok Bot, included on the $30 Premium tier of The X Subscription, the official X connector can read and search posts and check your mentions, but Andrew points out it has no tool for publishing. You connect a separate posting tool (he uses Postiz) to do that. His summary of the split is the best line in the guide: the connector reads, "the posting tool is the hands, and you are the brain."

The more important one: "only the Scheduler may post" is a rule written in a job description, not a lock. On Grok Bot, every Bot on your account shares one cloud computer, and xAI's docs warn not to treat separate Bots as a security boundary. So the real gate has to sit at account level. Andrew uses Grok Bot's "Ask first" rules for any scheduling, publishing, DM, or change to a scheduled post. Whatever tool you use, find its equivalent and switch it on before you build anything else.

The agents can find the conversation. You join it. That's the whole difference between an account that posts often and one that still sounds like a person.

What does a normal day look like?

Your part shrinks to a handful of decisions: pick an idea, approve a draft, confirm the post, write your own replies, and once a week decide what to change. One practical warning from the guide: approval requests raised by background routines expire if you don't answer them (about 30 minutes on Grok Bot), and then nothing goes out. Batch your approvals into a time you actually check.

How I run my version (and the piece I haven't built)

My setup isn't on Grok Bot, and it isn't as tidy as six named agents. It's a few scheduled tasks in Claude that share a playbook and a log. But it maps onto Andrew's design almost one to one:

  • The voice file is a playbook: who I am, the five groups of people I help, my offers, a bank of real client stories (anonymised, sector and country only), 40 post angles, and a list of words I never use.
  • The Scout and Writer are a weekday task that reads the news, picks an angle and a story, and sends me a LinkedIn draft plus, three days a week, an Instagram carousel.
  • The memory is a content log. Every post adds a line, so the system knows not to repeat an angle for three weeks or a story for seven days.
  • The gate is me. Drafts come to my phone. I post them myself.

Here's the honest part: I don't have an Analyst yet. My agents know what I've posted, but not what performed. That's the gap Andrew's design closes and mine doesn't, and it's the next thing I'm building. If you copy one idea from his guide before anything else, copy the Monday report. A team that never learns just publishes faster.

One more distinction I'd draw: my blog posts do publish on their own, because aicoachalex.com is my site and the only person whose rules apply is me. Social platforms are rented land with automation rules. That's where the yes stays human.

How do you build it? 8 steps for a small business

  1. Pick your platform and your posting tool. For most business owners that's LinkedIn first, then Instagram. Choose one posting tool that supports approval before publishing. Check its price separately: it's not included in your AI plan.
  2. Switch on the gate first. Before any agent exists, set an account-level rule: ask me before publishing, scheduling, changing a scheduled post or sending any message.
  3. Write the voice file from real posts, not adjectives. "Direct and witty" means nothing to a model. Your 20 best posts and 10 worst do. Use the second prompt below, then add a banned-words list by hand.
  4. Create only the Manager and the Scout. Live with the morning brief for a few days. If the ideas are wrong, fix the Scout's instructions, not the chat.
  5. Add the Writer, then run one full cycle by hand. Idea, three drafts, your pick, your edit, your yes. When something breaks, correct the agent's job description so the fix sticks.
  6. Automate one routine. Only the morning brief, only on weekdays. Keep routines rare: a schedule that fires every few minutes burns your usage for nothing.
  7. Adapt, don't copy, across platforms. The same text on LinkedIn and Instagram looks automated. Have the Writer rebuild each approved post for each platform, with no new claims. Add one platform at a time.
  8. Add the Analyst, and cap the spend. A weekly report, plus a hard usage limit. On shared usage plans, six agents chatting in one group conversation drain your allowance on messages that weren't meant for them. Talk only to the Manager.

Prompt 1: the Manager

Paste as the Manager's instructions 
You manage my social media team for [business name], which helps [who] with [what].
I only talk to you. You give each task to the right agent and bring me one clean result.

Team: Scout (ideas), Writer (drafts), Designer (visuals), Scheduler (posting tool), Analyst (weekly report).

Rules:
1. Nothing is scheduled, published, edited after scheduling, or sent as a message until I say yes in this chat.
2. Only the Scheduler uses the posting tool.
3. Never reply to comments, mentions or messages for me. List the ones worth answering; I reply myself.
4. One agent at a time unless a task truly needs two.
5. Every fact needs a source link. No source, no fact.
6. Keep your updates to me under 5 lines.

Prompt 2: build your voice file

Paste with your last 30 posts 
Below are my last 30 posts from [LinkedIn / Instagram], each with its likes and comments.

Create a file called voice.md with:
1. My 8 most common hook patterns, each with a real example from my posts
2. My typical post length and sentence length
3. Words and phrases I use often
4. Topics that get engagement and topics that flop
5. What my 5 best posts do that my 5 worst don't

Quote only my real posts. Don't describe me with adjectives.
End with an empty section called NEVER USE for me to fill in.

[paste posts here]

Prompt 3: the morning brief for a local business

Scout routine, weekdays 
Every weekday at 8:00, find 5 post ideas for [business] in [city / sector].
My customers are [who]. They care about [problems].

Look at the last 48 hours only: news in my sector, questions customers ask online, and claims I could disagree with using evidence.
For each idea give: one line, why it matters this week, and the source link.
Skip anything you can't link to. Skip engagement bait.
Send me a numbered list, then stop and wait for my pick.

What are you allowed to automate on social media?

Scheduling your own posts is fine, and so is using AI to help write them. The line is replies. X's rules say automated replies based on keyword searches alone are not permitted, and its developer guidelines require prior approval from X before an AI-powered app generates and posts replies. Break those rules and X may filter your posts from search or suspend your account.

If you live on LinkedIn or Instagram rather than X, don't assume you're freer. A review of ten platforms' rules by Blotato found none that clearly lets an AI agent reply to or message someone without the platform's approval or the other person going first, as of October 2026. The safe rule is the same everywhere: agents read and draft, you reply.

Setting that up, training the voice on the owner's real posts, and reviewing the Monday report with them for the first month is a concrete, visible piece of work. FAQ

What is an AI social media team?

A set of AI agents that each handle one part of social media: finding ideas, writing drafts in your voice, making visuals, scheduling approved posts and reporting on results, coordinated by one manager agent you talk to.

How much does Grok Bot cost?

Grok Bot is not sold on its own. Since October 2026 it is included in The X Subscription from the Premium tier at $30 a month, with a weekly usage allowance. A posting tool is a separate cost.

Can Grok Bot post to X by itself?

According to the guide this article builds on, the official X connector can read, search and check mentions but has no publishing tool, so you connect a separate posting tool such as Postiz. Keep an approval rule on publishing either way.

Is it allowed to automate posts on X?

Scheduling your own original posts and using AI to help write them is allowed. Automated replies triggered by keyword searches are not, and AI reply bots need prior approval from X. Rule-breaking automation can get posts filtered or accounts suspended.

How do I make AI posts sound like me?

Give the AI your real posts, not adjectives. Have it extract your hook patterns, length, phrases and what your best posts do differently, add a list of words you never use, and add a new rule each time you correct a draft.

Do I need all six agents to start?

No. Start with the Manager and the Scout, run the process by hand for about a week, then add the Writer, Designer, Scheduler and Analyst one at a time.
