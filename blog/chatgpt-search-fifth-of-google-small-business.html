OpenAI says ChatGPT now does a fifth of Google's searches. Can it find you?

OpenAI's head of ChatGPT's consumer product says its search-like queries are now roughly one-fifth of Google's volume, and more of them are about people, places and things. It's OpenAI's own estimate, not an audited number. But if your customers are starting to ask a chatbot instead of a search box, here's how to check what it says about your business, and fix it this week.

Short answer

On 3 November, OpenAI's ChatGPT consumer product lead, Adam Fry, said ChatGPT's volume of search-like queries is now roughly one-fifth of Google's. He counts any question where ChatGPT goes and searches the web, and says those searches have shifted towards looking up people, places and things. OpenAI gave no absolute numbers, so treat it as a company claim. He also said ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users and 250 million weekly voice users. For a small business, the practical move is simple: ask ChatGPT the questions your customers ask, see whether you show up and whether it gets your facts right, make sure OpenAI's search crawler is allowed on your site, and put your services, area, prices and FAQs on your website in plain text.

If you run a business, you've probably spent years thinking about Google: reviews, the map listing, page one. Here's the question I'd ask you this week instead: have you ever asked ChatGPT about your own business? Try it. You might not be there at all. Or you might be there with last year's prices.

I publish on this blog every day partly for this reason. My first clients came from a marketplace that doesn't let me share links, so my own site had to become the way people find me, whether they type into Google or ask a chatbot. Yesterday OpenAI put a rough number on how big the chatbot side has become.

The numbers come from a short fireside chat at an OpenAI dinner in New York on Monday night, published by Alex Kantrowitz in Big Technology on 3 November. The speaker was Adam Fry, who leads ChatGPT's consumer product. Here's what he said, in plain English.

Two caveats. First, this is OpenAI talking about OpenAI. 9to5Google points out that the definition leaves a lot of wiggle room, and that OpenAI didn't say where its Google figure came from. Second, a chatbot question isn't the same as a Google search: one conversation can trigger several web searches. Gizmodo did its own rough maths using a third-party estimate of 16 billion Google searches a day, which would put ChatGPT at around 3.2 billion search-like interactions daily. That's Gizmodo's estimate, not OpenAI's.

Even if the real figure is lower, the direction is clear. People are asking a chatbot the questions they used to type into Google, and the questions are increasingly about local, real-world things. "Who can fix a fuse box in my area this week?" is exactly that kind of question.

Your customers are asking a chatbot about you. Go and ask it first.

Why does this matter to your business?

  • The chatbot answer is the new shop window. When ChatGPT answers "best accountant for freelancers in Barcelona", the customer often sees three names and a short reason for each. If you're not one of them, you never got the click.
  • Wrong facts cost you quietly. If the answer quotes old prices, a closed office or a service you dropped, the customer decides before they ever reach you. Nobody tells you it happened.
  • Voice makes it a one-answer world. With 250 million people a week talking to ChatGPT, more questions get a spoken answer. A spoken answer rarely reads out ten options.
  • Your website matters more, not less. A chatbot that searches the web needs something clear to read. A site that says what you do, where, for whom and roughly for how much is easier to quote than a pretty site with the details hidden in images and PDFs. How do I get my business to show up in ChatGPT search?

  1. Ask the questions your customers ask. Write down five questions a real customer would type or say, in their words: "Who installs EV chargers near Sabadell?", "Is there a family dentist open on Saturdays in Clapham?" Run each one in ChatGPT with web search on. Note whether you appear, who appears instead, and what it says about you.
  2. Make sure OpenAI's search crawler can read your site. OpenAI's crawler documentation says sites need to allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt to appear in ChatGPT search answers. A separate bot, GPTBot, is for model training, and you can block that one while still allowing search. OpenAI says changes for search take around 24 hours. If you're not sure what your file says, my post on the robots.txt line that opts you out of AI training walks through it.
  3. Put your key facts on the page in plain text. What you do, the area you cover, who it's for, opening hours, how to book, and a price or a "from" price where you can. Not only in a PDF brochure or a picture of a price list.
  4. Write an FAQ page in your customers' words. Take the questions from your WhatsApp and email (AI can pull them out for you) and answer each one in two or three sentences. These are the kind of answers a chatbot can quote.
  5. Fix the facts everywhere else. Your map listing, directory profiles and social bios should say the same thing as your website. When sources disagree, you can't control which one an AI picks.
  6. Re-check once a month. Keep a simple log: date, question, did we appear, what was wrong. Ten minutes a month tells you whether the work is paying off.

Copy this prompt: your AI visibility check

Paste this into ChatGPT with web search on. It runs the customer questions for you and turns the answers into a short to-do list. Run it again next month and compare.

AI visibility check prompt 
Search the web and act like a customer looking for a business like mine.

My business: [name]
What we do: [e.g. electrical installations and repairs for homes and small offices]
Where: [town / area]
My website: [URL]

Step 1. Answer each of these customer questions as you normally would, with your sources:
1. [e.g. Who is a reliable electrician near me for an urgent repair?]
2. [question 2]
3. [question 3]
4. [question 4]
5. [question 5]

Step 2. Make a table: question | did my business appear (yes/no) | who appeared instead | what you said about my business.

Step 3. List every fact about my business you found online (services, area, hours, prices, contact) and mark anything that looks outdated, missing or inconsistent between sources.

Step 4. Give me the five most useful changes to make to my website or listings, in order of impact. Only suggest things based on what you actually found. Do not invent facts about my business.

Also today in AI

CrowdStrike spends about $495M to watch over AI agents

Cybersecurity company CrowdStrike says it will buy Fiddler AI, which monitors AI agents inside companies, and Dreadnode, which tests AI models and agents for security flaws before they go live. TechCrunch reports, citing The Wall Street Journal, that the deals are worth about $400 million and $95 million. Fiddler will become Falcon Observe, and Dreadnode's testing will feed Falcon Guardian.

What it means for you: big companies now treat AI agents like new staff who need supervision. You don't need a security platform to do the small-business version: keep a one-page list of every AI tool or agent you use, what it can access, and who checks its work. It's the same habit I recommended after hackers sent AI agents after 200 organisations.

Revolut cuts its coding-AI bill to about a tenth

The fintech Revolut moved about 2,000 engineers from paid AI APIs to an open model, GLM 4.5, running on its own 56 Nvidia B200 chips, according to StartupHub.ai's write-up of a conference talk. The reported cost is about one tenth of its previous Anthropic and OpenAI bill, but StartupHub notes that figure leaves out staff, cloud and monitoring costs, and only worked after heavy tuning.

What it means for you: don't buy GPUs. The lesson at your size is that the most expensive model isn't the default for every job. Use the top model for the hard thinking and a cheaper one for routine drafting and sorting. My comparison of Haiku vs Sonnet vs Opus shows how big that price gap can be.

Anthropic shares a way to prove which AI wrote an answer

Anthropic published research and open-source code for FlashProve, a "provable inference" system that uses cryptography to prove a given output came from a specific model, TechCrunch reports. The idea is to stop a provider quietly swapping in a cheaper model. Anthropic calls it intermediate progress, and there are no plans to deploy it at any lab yet.

How to use it: nothing to install today. But if you pay an agency or a software vendor for "AI-powered" work, ask them in writing which model they use and whether that can change. One day you may be able to check.

FAQ

Does ChatGPT really handle 20% of Google's searches?

That's OpenAI's own estimate. On 3 November 2026, ChatGPT's consumer product lead Adam Fry said its volume of search-like queries is roughly one-fifth of Google's. OpenAI didn't publish absolute numbers or say how it measured Google's volume, so it isn't independently verified.

What counts as a search in ChatGPT?

According to Fry, any question where ChatGPT goes and searches the web counts, which he described as basically anything people used to take to a traditional search engine. He said those searches are shifting towards lookups of people, places and things, plus finance and health questions.

How do I get my business to appear in ChatGPT search?

Allow OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot, in your robots.txt, then make your website easy to quote: services, area, hours, prices and contact details in plain text, plus an FAQ page in your customers' words. Keep your listings consistent and check monthly what ChatGPT says about you. None of this guarantees a mention, but it removes the most common blockers.

What is the difference between OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot?

OpenAI's documentation says OAI-SearchBot is used to show websites in ChatGPT's search answers, while GPTBot collects content that may be used to train its models. The settings are independent, so you can allow search and block training.

How many people use ChatGPT every week?

Fry said ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users, up from 1 billion in about 60 days, and that 250 million people a week use voice features such as dictation and voice mode.

Should small businesses stop caring about Google?

No. Google is still far larger by OpenAI's own estimate. The good news is that most of the work overlaps: a clear website with accurate facts and real answers to customer questions helps you in Google and in AI assistants.
