Claude now builds your spreadsheets. Should you leave Excel?

Anthropic is rolling out Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards and Claude Data to paid plans. Here's what each one does, what Sheets still can't do, and the safe way for a business owner or professional to try it this week without betting the master file on a beta.

Short answer

Anthropic started rolling out three new Claude tools on 10 November 2026: Sheets (a spreadsheet Claude builds and edits for you), Whiteboards (a shared board where Claude turns notes into actions) and Data (plain-language questions over your business data, answered with charts and dashboards). They're for paid plans, on by default for Team, and Sheets and Whiteboards are in beta. Sheets is good at drafting a working spreadsheet from a description, with formulas, charts and drop-downs, and exports to .xlsx or CSV. It can't yet do pivot tables or macros, stops at 100,000 cells and can't pull live data from your apps. Don't leave Excel. Use Claude Sheets to draft, test and explain, then keep your master file where it is.

Most of the business owners I coach don't have a software problem. They have a spreadsheet problem. A quote calculator only one person understands, a job tracker with three versions on three laptops, a cash-flow sheet nobody dares to touch.

Until now, my advice was always the same: describe the sheet to the AI, let it write the formulas, then paste them into Excel yourself. This week that copy-paste step started to disappear. Claude can now build the spreadsheet itself, inside the chat. That's useful, and it's also exactly the moment to slow down for ten minutes and read the small print.

On Tuesday 10 November Anthropic's Claude account announced on X that the three tools were starting to roll out that day, as reported by IT-Connect. Here's the plain-English version, pulled together from IT-Connect, Tom's Guide, Yahoo Finance and PBX Science.

What can't Claude Sheets do yet?

This is the part that decides whether it touches your real numbers. Tom's Guide and PBX Science both list the same limits, citing Anthropic's documentation.

  • No pivot tables. If your monthly report is built on pivots, it stays in Excel or Google Sheets.
  • No protected ranges or filter views. You can't lock the formula cells so a colleague doesn't overwrite them. For a shared sheet, that matters.
  • No Excel macros. Anything automated with VBA won't run.
  • A 100,000-cell ceiling. That sounds like a lot until you have 2,000 rows by 50 columns. A job log or a full year of invoices can hit it.
  • No live data from your connected apps. The sheet won't refresh from your accounting or job-management software. For live numbers, that's what Claude Data is for, and it needs a proper data connector.

Why it matters to you

  • The gap between "AI explained it" and "the file exists" just closed. Most people I coach get stuck at the paste-into-Excel step. Now a quote calculator, a project tracker or a simple forecast can exist in the time it takes to describe it.
  • Highlighted changes are the real feature. The danger with AI and spreadsheets was never the formula, it was not knowing what changed. Seeing exactly which cells Claude touched makes checking its work possible for a non-technical person.
  • Specificity still wins. "Make me a budget" gets you an average budget. Your columns, your rules and one real example row get you your budget. Same lesson as every other AI tool.
  • Your master file is a business asset. Beta tools change. Keep the version your business runs on somewhere you control, with a backup, and treat Claude Sheets as the workshop, not the warehouse.
Let AI build the first draft of the spreadsheet. Keep the master copy where you can lock it, back it up and find it next year.

How should a small business try Claude Sheets?

Here's the 30-minute test I'd run with a client this week.

  1. Check you have it. Look for Sheets in Claude on a paid plan. On Team it should be on by default. On Enterprise, ask your admin, because Sheets starts switched off. If it's not there yet, the rollout hasn't reached you.
  2. Pick a sheet you'd happily throw away. A quote calculator, a weekly job tracker, a simple 12-month cash forecast. Not payroll, not the master client list.
  3. Describe it like you'd brief a new assistant. The columns, the rules ("VAT at 21%", "discount over 10 hours"), the drop-down options and one real example row. Use the prompt below.
  4. Check every highlighted cell. Ask Claude to explain each formula in one plain sentence and to add a test row where you already know the right answer. If the test row is wrong, the sheet is wrong.
  5. Export and decide where it lives. Download the .xlsx, open it in Excel or Google Sheets, lock the formula cells there, and save it with your other business files.
  6. Try Whiteboards at your next team meeting. Let Claude group the notes and draft the actions, then confirm each owner and date out loud before anyone leaves. AI drafts the to-do list, people own it.
  7. Leave Claude Data for later unless you already have a data warehouse or a CRM with a connector. Most small businesses I work with will get more from cleaning their job categories and customer lists first. AI dashboards built on messy data just make the mess look professional.

Copy this prompt: build your first Claude Sheet

Paste this into Claude with Sheets turned on, fill in the brackets, and run it. It works in ChatGPT or Gemini too, as instructions for a sheet you build yourself.

First spreadsheet brief 
Build me a spreadsheet I can use in my business.

What it's for: [e.g. quoting jobs / tracking weekly jobs / 12-month cash forecast]
Who uses it: [me / my office manager / 3 technicians on their phones]
Columns I need: [list them, in order]
Rules: [e.g. VAT 21%; 10% discount over 10 hours; status can only be Quoted, Booked, Done, Paid]
Here is one real example row: [paste it]

1. Build the sheet with clear headers, drop-downs for any column with fixed options, and formulas for every calculated column.
2. Add a "Check" tab with 3 test rows where I can see the expected answer next to the calculated one.
3. Add a "How it works" tab explaining every formula in one plain sentence.
4. List anything you assumed that I didn't tell you, so I can confirm it.
Keep it under 2,000 rows and don't use pivot tables or macros, so I can export it to Excel.

After

Claude builds the sheet, highlights what it changed, and a test tab proves the numbers. You export it, lock the formulas and keep the master file safe.

FAQ

What is Claude Sheets?

Claude Sheets is a spreadsheet that Claude builds and edits for you from a description in the chat. It can create tabs, formulas, formatting, charts and drop-down menus, highlights the cells it changes, lets you share the sheet for editing and exports to .xlsx or CSV. Anthropic started rolling it out in beta on 10 November 2026.

Who can use Claude Sheets, Whiteboards and Data?

People on paid Claude plans, with a gradual rollout. On Team plans the tools are on by default. On Enterprise, Sheets and Whiteboards start switched off for the admin to enable, and Claude Data switches on by default from 19 November 2026, according to IT-Connect and PBX Science.

Can Claude Sheets replace Excel or Google Sheets?

Not yet. According to Anthropic's documentation as reported by Tom's Guide and PBX Science, it doesn't support pivot tables, protected ranges, filter views or Excel macros, has a 100,000-cell limit and can't pull live data from connected apps. Use it to draft and test a sheet, then export it and keep the master file in Excel or Google Sheets.

What is Claude Whiteboards for?

Running meetings and workshops. Team members add notes to a shared board, Claude groups them by theme and drafts action items with owners and dates, and you can ask it to expand part of the board or turn it into a document or presentation. Boards can be moved to Miro or Lucid.

What is Claude Data and do I need it?

Claude Data lets you ask questions about your business data in plain language and get charts, dashboards, written summaries or narrated videos. It needs a data connector such as BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift or Salesforce. If your numbers live in a few spreadsheets and an accounting app, start with cleaning that data and with Claude Sheets.

Is it safe to put business data in Claude Sheets?

Treat it like any other cloud tool in beta: start with a sheet that holds no sensitive personal data, check your plan's data settings, and keep the master copy and backups in the system your business already relies on. Share sheets only with the people who need them.
