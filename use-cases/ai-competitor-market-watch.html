The problem: competitor news arrives late, or by accident

The CEO's brief was one line: every Monday, tell me what my competitors did last week. The news existed. It was just in three places nobody had time to read.

Press

Thin and late

Local coverage of his market is sparse. By the time a story runs, competitors announced it days or weeks earlier.

LinkedIn

Where they announce first

Launches and partnerships appear on company pages first, and LinkedIn blocks AI tools from reading posts.

X

Fast, but noisy

Sales events and conference appearances show up on X before anywhere else, buried in noise.

Hearing about a rival's launch three weeks late means answering questions in meetings you should have walked into prepared.

How it works: one notebook, three specialists

Each tool does the one job it's best at. They all write to the same Google Sheet. One of them writes the final answer.

  1. Grok reads X. Every Sunday at 20:00 it writes competitors' X posts into the sheet as rows.
  2. Claude reads LinkedIn and the press. LinkedIn blocks AI tools, so Claude uses the Apify connector (harvestapi/linkedin-post-search, no LinkedIn login) on the competitors' official company pages, plus a lighter run on posts that mention them.
  3. One Google Sheet is the single source of truth. Same columns for every tool, and a reliability column that only accepts ✅ or ⚠️.
  4. Claude becomes editor-in-chief. It merges duplicates (the same news in two sources = ✅), keeps facts apart from interpretation, and flags any source that came back empty.
  5. The report arrives on its own. A Claude scheduled task runs every Monday at 07:00 and sends a phone notification.

The 4-lesson path

Four 50-minute lessons, one working system. No code.

Lesson 1

Claude vs Grok, same brief

Claude wrote the better report: clear structure, facts apart from opinion, every item ✅ or ⚠️. Grok found two moves that only existed on X. Each tool got a job.

Lesson 2

One shared sheet

Grok writes X rows on Sundays. Claude reaches LinkedIn through Apify. Claude itself proposed splitting one $0.29 search into two lighter runs.

Lesson 3

Claude as editor-in-chief

Merge, tag, write one report in the CEO's language. First run: 14 new rows for about $0.20.

Lesson 4

Autopilot

A scheduled task every Monday at 07:00 and a phone notification. The CEO's only job: open and read.

What broke, and how we fixed it

Grok's first Sunday run failed silently. No error, no rows. A quiet week and a broken pipe look the same. Fix: if the X rows are missing, the report says so at the top.

An X row arrived with "Visitor" in the reliability column. Fix: the column became a dropdown that only accepts ✅ or ⚠️.

The lesson: one AI tool rarely does everything. Give each tool the job it's best at, give them one shared notebook, and let one of them write the final answer.

Questions about AI competitor monitoring

What is AI competitor monitoring?

AI competitor monitoring means AI tools collect what your competitors publish (press, LinkedIn, X) into one place every week, tag each item as confirmed or to verify, and write you a short report. You read it; you don't run it.

How much does an automated market watch cost?

In this engagement the only extra cost is LinkedIn data through Apify: about $0.20 a week (around $0.002 per post), capped at $0.50 a week and $5 a month. Claude and Grok run on paid plans the client already had, and Google Sheets is free.

Can AI read my competitors' LinkedIn posts?

Most AI chat tools can't read LinkedIn posts directly. Claude can use the Apify connector, which collects public posts from company pages without a LinkedIn login. We only watch official company pages and posts that mention them, at low volume.

Is it allowed under LinkedIn and X terms?

Both platforms restrict automated collection, and their terms change. This setup only reads public posts, never logs in to anyone's account, never posts or messages, and keeps volumes low. Check the current terms for your own use, and ask your lawyer if your business depends on it.

What about data privacy?

Only public information about competitor companies is collected. The shared sheet lives in your own Google account, and the report goes only to you. Nothing about your own company or clients needs to be shared with the tools.

Which tools do I need?

Claude (writing, checking, merging and the weekly schedule), Grok (reading X), Apify (reaching public LinkedIn posts) and one Google Sheet. If you already pay for Claude and Grok, the rest is a few cents a week.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Everything is built with written instructions, a spreadsheet, connectors and scheduled tasks. This one was built in four 50-minute lessons with a CEO, not a developer.

Can the report be in my language?

Yes. The client's report is written in his own language, whatever language the sources are in.
